How it works

You confirm. It does the rest.

01

Brain-dump. It does the boring part.

Talk or type the mess in your head. It comes back as real tasks: sized, broken down, ordered. You tap yes, no, or tweak. No forms, no settings maze. Under five minutes from install to an actual plan.

02

Your day, built around what's sacred.

It reads your Google Calendar and treats appointments, pickups, and flights as immovable. It will never "optimize" your ride to the airport. Everything else flexes around the fixed points.

03

When the day breaks, it bends.

Fell behind? Say so. It re-thinks the whole rest of the day (drops what doesn't matter, shrinks what can shrink, moves what can wait) instead of shoving every task later until 9pm looks like a hostage situation. Don't like a move? Reject it, and the plan reflows around what you keep.

Fair questions

Things you're right to ask

Is this another "AI app"?

Here's the actual machinery: a language model parses your sentence, and a deterministic solver does the schedule math. That split is why a proposed plan can't move your fixed appointments or invent impossible times. You'll never be asked to "chat with your calendar." You'll mostly tap yes and no.

Is this a medical or therapy app?

No. Do Over is a planning tool — not treatment, therapy, or medical advice. It's built for how ADHD brains actually use planners, and it makes no clinical claims. It's also for anyone whose days simply refuse to follow scripts.

What about my existing calendar?

Google Calendar connects read-only on day one. Your events are the skeleton the day gets built around, and they are never moved without your explicit say-so. More integrations come after the core earns your trust.

Where does my data go?

Your brain-dump goes to a language model to be parsed, then to the solver. Calendar access is read-only. Nothing is sold, nothing trains anyone's models, and the specific providers and retention terms will be published plainly before the beta opens. If any of that changes, it changes here first.

And when Do Over joins the graveyard?

Then you lose nothing. Plans export as plain text or calendar files anytime, and because your Google Calendar was read-only all along, it never stopped being the source of truth.

Why would this one stick when the others didn't?

You shouldn't take that on faith — every headstone in the graveyard made the same promise. The honest difference: the others treat a broken day as your failure to plan. Do Over treats the broken day as the main event. Whether that's enough is exactly what the free trial (and the 60-day refund) is for.
